An enormous number of gift vouchers change hands every Christmas. That isn't
laziness: it's what's left after forty minutes of looking and finding nothing.

The problem isn't a lack of choice. It's the opposite.

## Start with the person, not the shop

Most of us open a shopping site and type "gift ideas for men in their 40s". It's
already lost at that point: the shop only knows products, and you have just
asked it to guess at someone it has never met.

Three questions beat an hour of browsing:

- **What do they do when nobody is asking anything of them?** That's where the
  real interests live, not in what they post.
- **What do they complain about and never fix?** Knives that don't cut, a
  reading lamp that was never bright enough, a bag that was never meant for this.
- **What would land worst?** One more object to store, something obviously
  generic, or something they already own.

The third question is the one everyone skips, and usually the most useful.

## Decide, then check

Once you have an idea, stop looking. The trap is going back to compare fifteen
variants and leaving with nothing. Commit, then verify only two things: what it
actually costs in a shop, and whether someone else has already given it.

## Where to buy in Switzerland

- **Galaxus** and **Digitec** for anything technical, tools, or kitchen.
- **Manor** for the home, the table, and things that wrap well.
- **Zalando** for clothes and shoes, with the size checked first.

Prices are in francs, and delivery windows tighten fast before Christmas: from
mid-December, count working days rather than days.

## The calendar matters more than the budget

A good gift bought on 23 December is an average gift. The real constraint is
almost never money. It's how much time you have left to change your mind.
